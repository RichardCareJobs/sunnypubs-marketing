Some drinks just belong in the sunshine. Lots of the world's best-loved cocktails were made in warm places, with cold, fresh, fruity flavours that suit a hot day. Here are eight of our favourites, each from a different country.

## 1. Aperol Spritz (Italy)

Bright orange and lightly bitter, the Aperol Spritz is Italy's favourite pre-dinner drink. Aperol was launched in 1919 by brothers Luigi and Silvio Barbieri at a trade fair in Padua, [according to Campari](https://www.camparigroup.com/en/news/2019-04-16/aperolr-toasts-100-years-joy). Mix it with prosecco and a splash of soda, and add a slice of orange.

**Why it works in the sun:** it's long, fizzy and lower in alcohol than most cocktails.

## 2. Pimm's Cup (England)

Pimm's was first made in London in the 1800s by James Pimm, who ran an oyster bar. Today it's the classic drink of an English summer, topped up with lemonade and packed with strawberries, cucumber, orange and mint.

**Why it works in the sun:** it's light and refreshing, and almost a fruit salad in a glass.

## 3. Sangria (Spain)

Sangria is red wine mixed with chopped fruit, a little brandy or orange liqueur, and something fizzy. It's usually made by the jug, so it's perfect for sharing at a long lunch outside.

**Why it works in the sun:** it's served icy cold and meant to be shared.

## 4. Mojito (Cuba)

From Havana, the Mojito is white rum, lime, sugar and fresh mint, topped with soda water over plenty of ice.

**Why it works in the sun:** the mint and lime make it one of the most refreshing drinks around.

## 5. Margarita (Mexico)

Tequila, lime juice and orange liqueur, served with a salt rim. Nobody is quite sure who invented it, but most stories place it in Mexico in the 1930s or 1940s. Its cousin, the Paloma, swaps in grapefruit soda.

**Why it works in the sun:** sharp, salty and citrusy, it's made for hot weather.

## 6. Caipirinha (Brazil)

Brazil's best-known cocktail is made with cachaça, a spirit distilled from sugarcane juice, muddled with lime and sugar. Brazil even protects the name in law, with a [2003 decree](https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/decret/2003/decreto-4851-2-outubro-2003-497513-publicacaooriginal-1-pe.html) setting out what a caipirinha is.

**Why it works in the sun:** it's simple, zesty and very cold.

## 7. Pisco Sour (Peru)

Pisco (a grape brandy), lime juice, sugar and egg white, shaken until frothy and finished with a few drops of bitters. It's linked to Morris' Bar in Lima, which opened in 1916. Chile also claims the drink, but Peru celebrates it with National Pisco Sour Day on the first Saturday of February, [according to Peru's tourism board](https://peru.info/en-us/gastronomy/news/2/12/national-pisco-sour-day-a-celebration-of-perus-iconic-cocktail).

**Why it works in the sun:** tangy and smooth, with a silky foam on top.

## 8. Singapore Sling (Singapore)

The Singapore Sling was created at the Raffles Hotel in Singapore in the early 1900s, and is usually credited to barman Ngiam Tong Boon. It's a pink, fruity mix of gin, cherry liqueur, pineapple juice and lime.

**Why it works in the sun:** it's tropical, sweet and a little bit fancy.

## Take it easy in the heat

A long, sunny afternoon can sneak up on you, so it pays to pace yourself:

- **Have water between drinks.** The heat dries you out faster than you think.
- **Go long.** Spritzes, Pimm's and sangria are topped up with something fizzy, so they're lighter than a short, strong cocktail.
- **Try them without the alcohol.** Most of these work well as alcohol-free versions. A mojito without the rum, or a spritz made with an alcohol-free aperitif, still tastes like summer.
