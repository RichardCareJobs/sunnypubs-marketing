Almost every country has its own version of the beer garden. The name changes, the drinks change and the rules change, but the idea is the same everywhere: a cold drink, a seat outside and a bit of sun. Here's what it's called in ten countries, and what it's like.

## 1. Germany: the Biergarten

This is where it all started. Munich's brewers planted chestnut trees over their beer cellars to keep them cool, then put tables underneath and started selling beer on the spot. (We told the full story in [why we love a drink in the sun](/blog/why-we-love-drinking-outdoors/).)

**What it's like:** long wooden tables shared with strangers, gravel underfoot and beer by the litre. In Bavaria you can still bring your own food to many beer gardens, a tradition that is even protected by state law.

## 2. England and Ireland: the pub garden

In England and Ireland it's usually called a beer garden or pub garden. It's often a lawn or courtyard tucked behind the local pub, with picnic benches and a few umbrellas.

**What it's like:** relaxed and a little scruffy, in the best way. The first sunny weekend of the year, every table is full by lunchtime.

## 3. France: la terrasse

The French don't really do beer gardens. They do the café terrasse: rows of small tables and chairs set out on the footpath, usually facing the street.

**What it's like:** it's all about people-watching. Order a coffee, a glass of wine or a small beer, and you can sit for hours without anyone rushing you.

## 4. Spain: la terraza

In Spain, a terraza can be a few tables on a town square or a rooftop above the city. Drinks usually come with a small snack, and in some cities the tapas are free.

**What it's like:** busy and social. Spaniards eat late, so the terrazas often fill up just as the sun starts to set.

## 5. Austria: the Schanigarten

Vienna's version is the [Schanigarten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schanigarten), a small outdoor seating area set up on the footpath outside a café, bar or restaurant. They traditionally appear in spring and are packed away for winter.

**What it's like:** a bit more polished than a German beer garden. Think coffee and cake in the afternoon, then a spritz or a glass of local wine later on.

## 6. Czech Republic: the zahrádka

Czechs drink more beer per person than almost anyone, so it's no surprise they love an outdoor drink. A beer garden is a pivní zahrada, but you'll often hear zahrádka, which means "little garden". Prague's Letná beer garden, on a hill above the river, has one of the best views in the city.

**What it's like:** simple and cheap, with plastic cups or heavy glass mugs. In many Czech pubs, the waiter keeps bringing fresh beers until you tell them to stop.

## 7. The Netherlands: the terras

The Dutch love sitting outside so much they have a saying for it: *een terrasje pakken*, or "grabbing a terrace". As soon as the sun appears, the canal-side terrasses fill up.

**What it's like:** cosy and chatty, with outdoor heaters and blankets to stretch the season. Order a small, foamy lager and a plate of bitterballen.

## 8. Japan: the rooftop beer garden

Japan borrowed the English name, but gave it a twist. Every summer, department stores and hotels open beer gardens on their rooftops.

**What it's like:** city views, plenty of food, and usually an all-you-can-drink deal for a set time. It's a popular after-work treat on a warm evening.

## 9. Australia: the beer garden

Australians call it a beer garden too, but it's often a courtyard, deck or rooftop rather than an actual garden. With so much sunshine, the big umbrellas and shade sails matter as much as the sun.

**What it's like:** casual and family-friendly, with a cold beer or a glass of local wine and a pub meal. On a warm weekend, it's the first place people head.

## 10. The USA: the patio

In the US, you're more likely to hear patio than beer garden. Most bars and restaurants with outdoor seating have one, from a few tables on the footpath to a big deck out the back. German-style beer gardens are popular too, especially in cities with German roots like Milwaukee and Cincinnati.

**What it's like:** relaxed, with a local craft beer and plenty of food. Patio season kicks off as soon as the weather warms up.

## Summing up

Whether it's a Biergarten, a terrasse or a rooftop in Tokyo, the best part is always the same: a cold drink in the sunshine. Wherever you are in the world, [Sunny](https://sunnypubs.app/) shows you the pubs and beer gardens in the sun right now, so you can find your sunny spot.
