There's a particular kind of Dublin evening — clear, still, the canal flat as glass — where the only real decision is which side of the water to be on. Sunny was built for exactly that decision, so we walked the southside with a notebook and a pint glass for science.

## Start at the canal, finish on a rooftop

Portobello does the early shift beautifully. The stretch along the Grand Canal catches full sun from mid-afternoon, and the benches outside the locks stay lit while the footpaths opposite have already cooled. If you're chasing the last of it, move uphill — Ranelagh's west-facing gardens are the ones still glowing at half seven.

> If it has a chair in the sun, we'll find it. Dublin just happens to hide its best chairs up a side street.

## Three to put on your list

- The lock-side benches in Portobello — full sun from 3pm, busy by 5.
- A west-facing Ranelagh garden that holds light until the sun drops behind the terraces.
- A Camden Street rooftop that's all shade at lunch and all gold by dinner.

The trick isn't finding sun at noon — anyone can do that. It's knowing where it lands at the exact hour you finish work. Open the map, check the live count, and let the afternoon plan itself.
